Overview

Abstract

Collecting data for manipulation with high-DOF hands is challenging, as interfaces must capture rich hand motion while rendering the contact interactions essential for precise manipulation. Existing data collection approaches face a trade-off: teleoperation ensures deployment consistency but lacks force feedback, while handheld (in-the-wild) systems provide natural force transparency but introduce a visual embodiment gap at deployment. We present DITTO, a Dexterous Interface for Transparent TeleOperation, which resolves this through the anatomically informed co-design of a dexterous 7-DOF robotic hand and a kinematically equivalent motorized exoskeleton. A 1-to-1 actuator mapping between the exoskeleton and robotic hand enables handheld (in-the-wild) data collection and bilateral teleoperation with joint-level force feedback unified in a single platform. We demonstrate that the DITTO exoskeleton spans the operator's natural index-to-thumb workspace, and showcase DITTO's dexterous capabilities via learned policies on contact-rich tasks.

Force Feedback

DITTO's leader-follower 1:1 joint correspondence enables joint-level force feedback.

Dexterous Teleoperation

DITTO enables highly dexterous teleoperation, leveraging coordinated index/thumb control.

Handheld (In-The-Wild) Data Collection

DITTO supports both:

  • Bilateral teleoperation with force feedback, for high-fidelity, robot-aligned data.
  • Handheld collection with the exoskeleton alone, for intuitive and scalable data collection.

Autonomous Policies

Policies trained on data collected via DITTO teleoperation perform contact-rich tasks relying on the hand's intrinsic degrees of freedom.

User Study

In a user study with 8 participants, DITTO's human-aligned kinematics and force feedback led to faster manipulation and gentler contact forces.

Experiment 1: Mahjong Tile Flipping

Flip a mahjong tile as many times as possible in 60 s, testing speed during coordinated index–thumb motion.

With the DITTO exoskeleton and force feedback, participants flipped the tile 2.2 times as often as with fingertip retargeting, and 1.3 times as often as with feedback turned off.

Every participant flipped the most tiles with the DITTO exoskeleton, and all but one did best with force feedback on. DITTO beats Fingertip (p ≤ 0.023, one-sided permutation test). Flips are each participant's total over the three trials; bars show the mean ± std across the 8 participants.

Experiment 2: Ratchet Turning

Turn a ratchet three full revolutions as fast as possible (instrumented with a force-torque sensor).

The robot arm is locked in place for this task, so any force on the sensor comes from the fingers alone.

Users took 30% less time using DITTO compared to fingertip retargeting. For this metric, performance was similar with and without force feedback.

With DITTO force feedback, users applied 47% less force, without significantly impacting task throughput.

This shows DITTO prevents undesired excessive forces that do not lead to better task performance but could damage the hardware.

With DITTO, 92.8% of force samples stayed under 14.8N (which is Fingertip's average contact force).

One-sided permutation tests: DITTO vs. DITTO-NF on both force metrics, p = 0.012; DITTO vs. Fingertip on every metric, p ≤ 0.023.
